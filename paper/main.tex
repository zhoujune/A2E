\title{From Authorization to Effects: Machine-Checked Recovery Semantics for
AI Tool Calls}

% Double-anonymous submission: author and affiliation fields are intentionally omitted.
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Authorization establishes that an AI tool call may occur, but it does not
establish what external effect occurred when a crash loses the call's reply.
A recovering agent runtime may therefore duplicate a permitted mutation,
report a false failure, or return success without durable evidence. We define
\emph{authorization-to-effect consistency}, a correctness contract relating
durable authority, physical attempts, recovery-stable terminal outcomes, and
adapter-specific external effects. We design a durable effect-broker protocol
and machine-check its composition through Broker, atomic-Journal, typed-WAL,
operational-adapter, and protected-service models. For every finite execution
admitted by our serialized fail-stop model, invocations have durable
authorization ancestry, terminals are unique, accepted results have exact
provenance, and committed history grows monotonically across recovery; a
recovery-time \Commit must cite an exact successful outcome already durable at
the crash boundary. For covered
\Idempotent and \Deduplicated protected executions satisfying explicit
adapter, persistence, coverage, and closed-interface premises, protected
effects are mediated and terminal outcomes refine the declared effect law.
Unresolved \Uncontrolled effects are not retried and remain \Unknown. The
artifact includes a verified executable typed-record kernel and injects its
compiled gate into selected Rust-broker append and replay paths for
representative traces. A fail-closed submission profile additionally requires
an immutable manifest and that gate for every evaluation broker construction,
with attested preview/commit, replay, and recovery closure.
Fault injection, proof-effort measurements, and local microbenchmarks exercise
the design. This integration narrows, but does not close, the whole-Rust
implementation-refinement gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Consider an AI agent that calls
\texttt{append\_report\_entry("/workspace/}\\
\texttt{report.log", entry)}. The user has
approved writes to that file, and a capability monitor or resource sandbox
correctly authorizes the request. The protected service appends the entry, but
its reply is lost before the broker persists an outcome; the broker then
crashes. On recovery, reporting \Fail may be false because the append may be
visible. Reporting \Commit lacks durable result evidence. Blind retry may
append the entry twice. Authorization has done its job, yet the system still
does not know what it may soundly tell the agent.

This ambiguity is a classical distributed-systems problem, not a new failure
mode created by AI. The agent tool boundary makes several familiar obligations
meet in one place. The agent chooses tools and parameters dynamically;
authority is scoped, budgeted, and historical; tools expose heterogeneous
effect semantics; one logical action may create several physical attempts; and
the recovered terminal result becomes input to later autonomous planning. A
wrong terminal classification can therefore authorize a later action on a
false premise even when every individual tool invocation stayed inside its
resource sandbox.

Existing mechanisms solve important local pieces. Capability systems and
agent sandboxes constrain accessible resources~\cite{watson2010capsicum,buehler2026agentbound}.
Durable workflow engines replay local history but require application-level
assumptions about external activities~\cite{temporal2026workflow,temporal2026activity}.
Idempotency keys and exactly-once RPC schemes let a cooperating service
recognize repeated requests~\cite{lee2015rifl,jena2025idempotency,stripe2026idempotency}.
Crash-safe storage preserves a log. None of these guarantees alone proves that
a terminal conclusion returned at an agent boundary corresponds to the same
authorized logical request, its physical attempts, and its surviving external
effect across the complete crash/recovery history. The open problem is their
composition, not the absence of the individual building blocks.

We study two questions:

\begin{description}
  \item[Correctness question.] What may an agent tool broker soundly conclude
    about an authorized external effect after a crash makes its outcome
    ambiguous?
  \item[Engineering question.] How can a broker enforce that contract across
    durable storage, retries, heterogeneous tools, and a protected service,
    and how much of the design can be connected to executable code?
\end{description}

Our answer is \emph{authorization-to-effect consistency}. A terminal outcome
must be derived jointly from durable authorization and attempt evidence and
from an explicit tool-effect contract. \ReadOnly, \Idempotent, and
\Deduplicated tools permit different forms of repetition. An \Uncontrolled
tool supplies no repetition law, so an unresolved attempt cannot be retried
and terminates as \Unknown. Importantly, \Unknown makes no negative claim
about an unpersisted physical outcome: its abstract semantics permits zero or
one effect. In the lost-reply example, the durable prefix alone cannot select
between them.

We realize this contract with an append-only effect broker. The protocol makes
authorization and physical-attempt intent durable before invocation, assigns
distinct attempt identifiers, rejects stale deliveries, and permits at most
one durable terminal per logical request. We then prove the protocol in layers:
an abstract Broker, an atomic Journal, a typed write-ahead log (WAL), an
operational adapter, and an independently defined protected-service execution.
The proof separates guarantees that hold for every admitted finite history from
protected-effect guarantees that additionally require a valid adapter,
execution coverage, typed persistence, and a closed interface with no bypass.

The retained proof suite verifies 72 of 72 registered Verus targets, comprising
1,511 dependency-aware non-duplicated obligations. A bounded TLA+ suite passes
13 of 13 retained scenarios. A verified executable kernel accepts arbitrary
legal sequences of eight supported durable record types, including terminal
failure and uncertainty records. A kernel-in-the-loop harness calls the
compiled proof-erased kernel from selected Rust-broker append and replay paths
and checks exact prefixes, log sequence numbers, terminal state, recovery
classification, and capability budget. The submission evaluation removes the
ungated opener and retains a versioned gate attestation. This is stronger than
a disconnected model and prototype, but it is not whole-program refinement of
the Rust broker.

This paper makes three contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item We define authorization-to-effect consistency: a crash-aware contract
    connecting durable capability ancestry, attempt and delivery provenance,
    recovery-stable outcomes, explicit uncertainty, and adapter-relative
    external effects at an AI tool boundary.
  \item We give a compositional machine-checked protocol theorem from Broker
    history through Journal and typed WAL to covered protected-service
    executions. The theorem states its serialization, persistence, coverage,
    and closed-interface premises explicitly and does not advertise generic
    physical exactly-once execution.
  \item We provide a verified executable record kernel, kernel-in-the-loop
    integration with a Rust reference broker, a 21-case crash matrix, an
    18-cell semantic fault classification, proof-effort data, and repeated
    runtime measurements across several storage paths.
\end{itemize}

The novelty is not any one of capabilities, WAL recovery, idempotency,
deduplication, or \Unknown. It is their machine-checked semantic composition
from historical authority to the external effect and terminal conclusion used
by an agent.

\section{The Authorization-to-Effect Gap}
\label{sec:gap}

\subsection{The agent tool boundary}

Our setting contains five roles. An \emph{agent} proposes a tool call. A policy
or capability issuer supplies trusted authorization metadata. A serialized
\emph{broker} admits the logical request and controls its physical attempts. A
durable store retains broker records. An \emph{adapter} interprets delivery and
retry behavior for a protected service that owns the external effect. The
broker eventually exposes a terminal outcome that the agent can inspect.

We distinguish a \emph{logical request} $r$ from each \emph{physical attempt}
$(r,a)$. The distinction is essential: recovery may issue attempt $a+1$ for
the same logical action, yet the protocol must not silently turn those attempts
into several logical outcomes. Authorization is also historical. It is not
enough that a capability exists after recovery; each physical invocation must
descend from the exact durable authorization and request description that
preceded it.

\subsection{Authorized append, lost reply}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeline} follows the example used throughout the paper. The
broker durably records authority and a physical start, then invokes the
service. The append may linearize before the reply is lost. At the recovery
point, the durable prefix establishes that an authorized attempt began, but it
does not reveal on which side of service linearization the crash occurred.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/authorized-effect-timeline.pdf}
  \caption{An authorized append crosses an ambiguity window. The same durable
  prefix permits different recovery actions only because the declared effect
  contracts provide different external laws.}
  \Description{Timeline from durable authorization and start through service
  linearization, lost reply, and crash. Recovery branches into stable-key
  deduplication, law-governed idempotent retry, or no retry and Unknown for an

For a service that satisfies the \Deduplicated contract in our running example,
the service atomically stores both the append and a decision under stable key
$k$. Retrying with $k$ retrieves that decision
without a second append, provided the memo survives every possible retry. For
an \Idempotent adapter, retry is justified only if repeated calls refine the
same abstract mutation. For an \Uncontrolled adapter, the first attempt may
already have changed the world and no contract relates a second attempt to it;
the broker therefore refuses retry and records \Unknown.

Notice what \Unknown means. It does not mean that no append occurred and makes
no negative assertion about an unpersisted result. In this lost-reply prefix,
the durable evidence alone cannot choose between zero and one effect.
Preserving both interpretations is safer than manufacturing a Boolean answer
for the agent.

For example, a correct WAL may preserve a 
\texttt{Start} record while saying nothing about the service state. Conversely,
a correctly memoized service key says nothing about whether the originating
call was authorized or whether a stale response was attached to another
attempt. A resource sandbox can prevent writes outside
\texttt{/workspace/report.log}, yet both a single append and a duplicate append
remain inside the permitted path. The desired property quantifies across all
of these components and over history, rather than checking one recovery branch
in isolation.

\subsection{Assumptions and non-goals}

We model arbitrary finite histories with one serialized WAL writer and one
executor slot. Crashes are fail-stop; recovery may itself crash. Configurations
are well formed, capability policies and initial budgets are trusted inputs,
and the formal WAL manipulates typed full or torn frames with atomic modeled
truncation. Protected-effect results assume a closed formal interface in which
the protected service cannot be invoked out of band.

The paper proves safety, not liveness, fairness, or bounded recovery time. It
does not model Byzantine components, concurrent writers, global
linearizability among multiple requests, or correctness of arbitrary external
tools. It does not refine byte encoding, CRCs, \texttt{fsync}, filesystems,
networks, operating-system isolation, or the delivery of a terminal result back
to the agent. These exclusions are not hidden implementation details: they
define where our end-to-end claim stops.

\section{Authorization-to-Effect Consistency}
\label{sec:contract}

We define the correctness contract before presenting the protocol. Let
$\tau$ be a finite execution, $J(\tau)$ its durable record history,
$P_r(\tau)$ the request-local physical history for request $r$, and
$T_r(\tau)$ the set of durable terminal records for $r$. A terminal is one of
$\Commit(a,v)$, $\Fail(a)$, or $\Unknown(a,q)$, where $a$ is an optional or
selected attempt, $v$ is a validated value, and $q$ is a reason-specific
evidence category.

\subsection{Historical safety obligations}

Authorization-to-effect consistency first requires properties independent of
the service's effect law:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Durable authorization ancestry.} Every physical invocation
    has a unique earlier durable \texttt{Start}; that start descends from a
    valid durable \texttt{Authorize}, and a successful append acknowledgement
    covers the start before invocation.
  \item \textbf{Scope and budget conservation.} The request descriptor is
    immutable and the selected capability covers its resource and arguments.
    Each authorization consumes one capability-budget unit; physical attempts
    separately obey the request's attempt bound and retry discipline.
  \item \textbf{Exact delivery provenance.} An accepted delivery names the
    active request and attempt and thereby identifies the unique prior
    invocation and its canonical call descriptor. A successful value satisfies
    the request predicate and is copied exactly through its durable outcome to
    \Commit; stale and mismatched deliveries do not change durable state.
  \item \textbf{Terminal uniqueness and provenance.} $|T_r(\tau)|\leq 1$.
    A \Commit names a prior durable successful outcome with the exact delivered
    value. A \Fail names durable failure evidence sufficient under the adapter
    contract. A \Unknown names a legal durable evidence anchor and reason.
  \item \textbf{Recovery preservation.} Committed logical history grows only
    when a \texttt{Commit} record linearizes. Across recovery it is monotone,
    and every recovery-time \Commit must cite the exact successful
    \texttt{Outcome} and outcome LSN already present at the selected crash
    boundary. If recovery appends no \Commit, the histories at \texttt{Crash}
    and the first subsequent \texttt{FinishRecover} are equal.
\end{enumerate}

These are temporal properties. A large decision tree could implement the same
record constructors, but branch coverage would not prove that references remain
valid across every admitted history, that stale deliveries stutter, or that a
WAL recovery trace refines the same Broker history. Those relationships are
the subject of the mechanization.

\subsection{External-effect obligation}

Broker-history correctness is necessary but insufficient. We additionally
relate $P_r(\tau)$ to an external run $E_r$ supplied by the adapter and
protected-service semantics. At a coarse cardinality level, a terminal permits
the following abstract interpretations:

\[
  \mathcal{I}(r,o) =
  \begin{cases}
    0 & o=\Commit(a,v) \land r\text{ is \ReadOnly},\\
    1 & o=\Commit(a,v) \land r\text{ is mutating},\\
    0 & o=\Fail(a),\\
    \{0,1\} & o=\Unknown(a,q).
  \end{cases}
\]

The actual mechanization uses state relations rather than only cardinalities:
\ReadOnly also requires a valid observation; \Idempotent repetitions must
equal one abstract state update; and \Deduplicated executions must share one
stable service decision. A \Fail is allowed only when its evidence rules out a
protected mutation under the class law. The lost-success/then-failure case is
therefore \Unknown, not \Fail.

We separate two external properties. \emph{Effect refinement} says that the
terminal outcome and the full physical history denote an abstract effect
allowed by the adapter. \emph{Complete mediation} says that every protected
call in the modeled service execution comes from the broker's invocation
trace. Complete mediation is derived only inside a closed formal alphabet; it
is not a claim about production credentials, file descriptors, or network
routes.

\subsection{Adapter-relative recovery}

Table~\ref{tab:adapters} gives the four contracts. The retry decision is not a
property of a tool name. It is a semantic promise that an adapter and its
service must establish.

The \Deduplicated contract includes a retention premise: the service-owned
memo must outlive every retry and recovery interval during which the broker may
reuse the key. Expiring it earlier voids the guarantee. Likewise, idempotence
is a property of the protected abstract effect, not merely of an HTTP method or
adapter label. The contract promises at most one \emph{abstract} mutation; it
does not promise one physical invocation.

\section{Durable Effect-Broker Protocol}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Architecture and evidence boundaries}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the executable architecture and its proof

persists a checksummed byte WAL. In parallel, the machine-checked development

The K4 executable kernel is proved against a supported typed-record subset.
K4-I0 injects a gate into the broker's selected append and replay paths, so the

it remains a run-time conformance check, not a proof that all Rust paths refine

The capability policy, adapter classification, and protected-service law are
trusted semantic inputs. The broker enforces those inputs but does not infer
them from arbitrary code. Deployment must additionally ensure that protected
credentials or handles cannot bypass the broker if the complete-mediation
claim is to apply.

digest, retry class, and optional deduplication key. The protocol's durable

An invocation is enabled only after the \texttt{Start} is covered by a durable
append acknowledgement. Its invocation identifier is the start's log sequence
number. A delivery must name the occupied executor slot and the exact attempt;
an old delivery after retry is rejected without a WAL append. The capability
budget bounds admission; the request attempt bound and retry class bound
retries. Revocation is durable and prevents future authorizations, but does not
retroactively invalidate a request that was already authorized. The executable
K4 subset described in Section~\ref{sec:implementation} does not yet cover the
\texttt{Revoke} record.

\subsection{Crash and recovery rules}

A crash discards the volatile ready, in-flight, and delivered state while
preserving the acknowledged durable prefix. WAL replay reconstructs request
phase, attempts, capability budget, and any durable outcome. A full final frame
is replayed; a typed torn tail is ignored and truncated in the formal model.

Recovery can repair a conclusive durable failure to \Fail. A persisted
successful \texttt{Outcome} can be terminalized as \Commit during recovery,
but only with the exact request, attempt, value, and outcome reference already
present in the crash prefix. An unmatched \texttt{Start} becomes
durable ambiguity. For \ReadOnly,
\Idempotent, or \Deduplicated, the broker may create another \texttt{Start}
only if the class law and remaining attempt budget permit it. For
\Uncontrolled, recovery appends \Unknown and never invokes again. Recovery
steps are themselves durable, so a second crash resumes from the repaired
prefix rather than reinterpreting volatile state.

The Rust broker resolves a durable successful outcome before considering the
conservative uncertainty branch, and the K4 gate checks the resulting \Commit
while still in \texttt{Recovering} mode. Its proved resume guard permits
\Idempotent and \Deduplicated Armed requests to resume, while
\Uncontrolled (and any \ReadOnly request) may resume only before a durable
Start exists. If no safe resume predicate holds, the demonstrated
\Uncontrolled profile terminalizes a still-\texttt{Armed} request as
\texttt{Unknown(Recovery)} using its latest durable Start/Outcome/Arm evidence.
This does not make the surrounding Rust control flow or byte WAL a verified
refinement.

In the running example, the first \Deduplicated attempt uses stable key $k$.
After the lost reply, recovery records ambiguity and starts attempt two with
the same key. The service returns the memoized success, which is persisted as
\texttt{Outcome} and then \Commit. The logical request has two physical calls
but one service decision and one protected append. If the adapter is
\Uncontrolled, the same durable prefix yields \Unknown without a second call.

\subsection{Design invariants}

The proof consumes four groups of invariants. Record references are typed,
strictly backward, and request-local. Append calls, linearization, and returns
form serialized crash-delimited prefixes. Physical events agree with durable
attempt state and use unique invocation identifiers. Finally, replay-derived
phase, terminal, budget, and recovery state agree with the stored records.
These invariants turn each recovery rule from a local branch into a statement
about the complete causal history.

\section{Mechanized Composition}
\label{sec:proof}

\subsection{Semantic layers and observations}

The mechanization separates five transition systems. The \emph{Broker}
contains logical requests, durable evidence, physical history, and volatile
control state. The \emph{atomic Journal} makes append call, linearization, and
return explicit. The \emph{typed WAL} adds full and torn frames, flush cuts,
scan, tail truncation, disk-full behavior, crash, and recovery. An
\emph{operational adapter} interprets broker events and service
linearizations. An independently generated \emph{protected-service execution}
owns accepted calls, decisions, returns, and external effect state.

The refinement observes projections rather than requiring lockstep event
identity. Projections retain authorization, journal records, physical calls,
logical terminals, append I/O, and request-local adapter histories. Private WAL
events such as scanning a torn tail may stutter. Monotone index maps relate
every source prefix to a target prefix, which is important because a crash can
interrupt any layer between visible events.

\subsection{Broker safety and persistence refinement}

At the Broker layer, induction over arbitrary finite executions proves the
historical obligations from Section~\ref{sec:contract}: durable authorization
ancestry, capability bounds, unique attempts, stale-delivery rejection,
terminal uniqueness, and exact committed-value provenance. The atomic Journal
weakly simulates this Broker while preserving every normalized projection. The
typed WAL weakly simulates the Journal. Its parsed acknowledged prefix remains
a prefix of all later media, including after a torn write, crash, aborted scan,
and tail truncation.

The two simulations compose. A storage-parametric program context can observe
only a masked endpoint view and synchronized visible event deltas. For every
plugged typed-WAL execution admitted by such a context, the construction
produces a plugged Broker execution with equal context state and view at every
mapped prefix. This is forward contextual replacement, not bidirectional
equivalence.

Committed-history preservation is then proved as a separate abstraction. Only
durable \texttt{Commit} linearization extends the history; all other actions
stutter. From a crash through the first later \texttt{FinishRecover}, committed
history is a prefix of the recovered history. Every intervening recovery
\Commit is proved to cite the exact successful \texttt{Outcome} and outcome
LSN already present at the crash prefix. The earlier equality result remains a
corollary for episodes with no recovery \Commit. Recovery may also append
\Fail or \Unknown and change control state, so neither result claims full-state
equality.

The recovery decision boundary is explicit rather than an implementation-side
error-code convention. For a typed durable record $q$,

\[
  D(q) =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathsf{Commit} & q = \texttt{CommitRec},\\
    \mathsf{Fail} & q = \texttt{FailRec},\\
    \mathsf{Unknown} & q = \texttt{UnknownRec},\\
    \mathsf{NotRecovery} & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]

Every record enabled while the machine is not \texttt{Online} is proved to
have one of the first three classes. Only the \mathsf{Commit} branch carries a
successful-value obligation, and its guard is equivalent to the pre-state
containing the matching durable \texttt{Outcome}; \mathsf{Fail} and
\mathsf{Unknown} are terminal repairs with no fabricated effect value. The
one-step decision theorem composes this guard with the committed-history delta,
which is the premise used by the general recovery theorem.

\subsection{From attempts to protected effects}

Figure~\ref{fig:composition} shows where the external premises enter. An
operational adapter execution is coupled event by event to an independent
protected-service execution. Broker invocation becomes a service call, a
service linearization changes protected state, and a delivery becomes a return.
At every related prefix, accepted service calls equal the WAL invocation
projection and adapter effect state agrees with protected effect state. Because
the service event alphabet exposes no raw target-mutation action, the coupled
closed execution derives complete mediation rather than assuming an arbitrary
matching trace.

  \centering

  \caption{Proof composition. History guarantees hold for all admitted finite
  serialized fail-stop histories. Protected-effect guarantees additionally
  require a covered operational/protected member and the marked adapter,

  \Description{Broker, Journal, and typed WAL form a history refinement chain.
  Typed WAL is coupled to adapter and protected-service executions. Coverage

The generic request-family theorem is deliberately coverage-conditioned. For
each terminal request in the shared WAL, a coverage function must supply a
valid operational/protected member with the required prefix product and effect
law. The proof then transports that member through the storage context to the
canonical Broker execution. Production Rust code does not automatically
construct these members; establishing that construction would be a further
implementation-refinement result.

To make the conditional boundary inspectable, we use the following paper-level
abbreviation for the exact predicates defined in the mechanization:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Covered}_C(r,o,s) \triangleq{}&
  \mathsf{WF}(o) \land \mathsf{WF}(s)
  \land \mathsf{PrefixCoupled}_C(o,s)\\
 &\land \mathsf{TypedPersist}_C(s)
  \land \mathsf{AdapterLaw}_C(r,o)
  \land \mathsf{Context}_C(s)
  \land \mathsf{Closed}_C(s).
\end{aligned}
\]

Here $o$ is the operational-adapter execution and $s$ is the independently
defined protected-service execution. `PrefixCoupled` equates the accepted-call
and broker-invocation projections at every related prefix;
`TypedPersist` transports the typed-WAL/Broker representation and terminal
evidence; `AdapterLaw` is the class-specific `Refines` relation over attempts,
external pre/post-state, and the logical outcome; `Context` is the
storage-parametric mapped-prefix relation; and `Closed` restricts target mutation
to the protected-service alphabet. The effect conclusion is the separate
predicate $\mathsf{Refines}_C(r,o,s)$, not a consequence of the word
``covered'' alone. A coverage function must return $(o,s)$ and discharge every
conjunct for each requested terminal; the two concrete witnesses prove
non-vacuity, while the family theorem leaves that construction as an explicit
implementation obligation.

\subsection{Main theorem}

We state the paper-facing synthesis below. Its two clauses have different
premises so that the protected-effect conclusion cannot be mistaken for an
unconditional property of arbitrary deployments.

\begin{theorem}[Authorization-to-effect consistency]
Let $C$ be a well-formed configuration and $\tau$ any finite execution admitted
by the serialized fail-stop Broker/Journal/typed-WAL semantics.

\emph{History clause.} Every physical invocation in $\tau$ has a unique prior
acknowledged \texttt{Start} and valid durable authorization; capability scope,
budget, and retry bounds hold; every accepted delivery has exact
request/attempt/value provenance; every logical request has at most one durable
terminal; every \Commit has exact successful-delivery provenance; and
committed history grows monotonically from a crash through the first subsequent
completed recovery. Any \Commit added during that interval has an exact
successful-\texttt{Outcome} value and reference already durable at the crash;
when the interval contains no \Commit, its endpoint histories are equal.

\emph{Protected-effect clause.} Additionally fix a terminal request $r$. If
$r$ is covered by a valid operational-adapter/protected-service execution
member satisfying the typed-persistence relation, adapter law,
storage-parametric-context relation, prefix coupling, and closed-interface
premise, then the protected call trace equals the broker invocation projection,
the source WAL and canonical Broker select the same terminal, and that terminal
refines the adapter's abstract effect semantics.
\end{theorem}

The first clause quantifies over every admitted finite history. The second
clause is instantiated for the modeled \Idempotent \textsc{EnsureMember} and
\Deduplicated keyed-slot developments; the latter is extended to a
coverage-conditioned request family. The current \ReadOnly instance reaches
adapter/WAL/Broker refinement but not the protected complete-mediation
endpoint. For \Uncontrolled, the history theorem proves at most one invocation;
an unresolved armed attempt is not retried and may terminate only as
\Unknown. No clause states physical exactly-once execution, result delivery to
the agent, liveness, or global ordering among different requests.
The history clause gives durable authorization ancestry to each corresponding
broker invocation. The concrete \Idempotent and \Deduplicated protected
witnesses additionally export durable-authorization provenance for their
protected decisions; we do not attribute that stronger export to the generic
coverage-family theorem.

\subsection{Non-vacuity and proof assurance}

The mechanization includes concrete witnesses rather than only conditional
interfaces. The \Idempotent \textsc{EnsureMember} witness linearizes attempt
one and loses its success across a crash; attempt two returns failure. Because
the first effect may have happened, \Fail is unsound. The trace terminates
\Unknown while the protected state contains exactly one abstract insertion.
The \Deduplicated keyed-slot witness lets attempt one create an atomically memoized
decision, crashes before delivery, retries with the same key, retrieves success,
and commits with exactly one protected mutation. Both witnesses establish that
the assumptions admit the ambiguity patterns the theorem is designed to cover.

The retained Verus run passes all 72 registered targets with zero reported
errors. It records 1,511 dependency-aware non-duplicated obligations and
38,952 summed target obligations. The runner verifies an exact read-only source
snapshot, validates complete source/import registration and dependency-parent
order, and binds source and toolchain hashes into its report. These counts and
integrity checks are assurance metadata, not independent correctness claims.
The TLA+ suite additionally passes 13 of 13 retained bounded scenarios; model
checking supplies finite state-space exploration, not an unbounded proof.

\section{Executable Realization and Refinement Boundary}
\label{sec:implementation}

\subsection{Rust reference broker}

The artifact includes a dependency-free standard-Rust reference broker. It
generates monotone request identifiers, validates capability budgets, optionally
binds admission to an immutable manifest, owns one executor slot, and exposes
the lifecycle from Section~\ref{sec:architecture}. Its byte WAL stores
versioned length frames with CRC32 checksums, appends and calls
\texttt{sync\_data}, discards an incomplete or checksum-invalid final frame,
and rejects corruption before the final frame. A configuration sidecar binds
capabilities, budgets, and the optional admission manifest across reopen.

The implementation includes \Idempotent, \Deduplicated, and \Uncontrolled
example adapters. The deduplication example keeps decisions in a service object
that survives broker restart, modeling an independently durable service;
restarting that object would violate its contract. These adapters and the byte
WAL are executable evaluation vehicles, not verified production components.

\subsection{Verified executable record kernel}

K4-A2 is the ordinary-record base of a verified executable typed-record kernel.
For every well-formed
finite manifest, it accepts any structurally legal sequence over
\texttt{Authorize}, \texttt{Prepare}, \texttt{Arm}, \texttt{Start},
\texttt{Outcome}, and \texttt{Commit}. It materializes missing request and
capability summaries from manifest budgets and proves that executable guards
and mutations preserve the abstract replay state, Journal projection,
one-based log sequence number, acknowledgement cut, terminal state, and budget.
Its retained target reports 326 verified obligations and zero errors.

K4-A3 extends the same invariant and append pipeline to \texttt{Fail} and
\texttt{Unknown}. Its executable guards prove retry-class-specific failure
conclusiveness and all five uncertainty reasons against abstract replay, while
exact references bind each terminal to its Outcome, latest attempt evidence,
or Arm record. It mutates concrete state to \texttt{Failed} or
\texttt{Unknown} and includes full append witnesses for Idempotent failure and
Uncontrolled recovery uncertainty. K4-A3 reports 347 verified obligations,
21 beyond K4-A2. K4-A4 adds executable \texttt{Online}/\texttt{Crashed}/
\texttt{Recovering} control, preserves the typed durable prefix across a
crash, and permits only terminal \texttt{Commit}/\texttt{Fail}/\texttt{Unknown}
decisions while recovering. Its durable-success witness appends \texttt{Commit},
proves exact begin/finish recovery control, and proves the class-sensitive
resume guard: \Idempotent/\Deduplicated may resume, while \Uncontrolled/\ReadOnly
may resume only before a durable \texttt{Start}. The K4-A4 target reports 365
obligations. \texttt{Revoke}
and byte persistence remain outside this layer.

\subsection{Kernel-in-the-loop integration}

K4-I0 compiles the proof-erased K4-A4 library, builds the ordinary Rust broker,
and links both into one harness. The harness injects an \texttt{AppendGate} into the
broker's real append and replay paths: every record passes K4 preview before
the WAL write, and K4 state is committed only after the WAL returns its durable
LSN. A fresh gate replays every durable prefix before serving requests. The
executions cover an eight-record \Idempotent crash/reopen/retry Commit, a
six-record manifest-bound \Deduplicated Commit with a full replay, conclusive
Fail, four reason-specific Unknown classifications (AmbiguousOutcome,
InvalidResult, Exhausted, and NonConclusiveFailure), an ordinary Uncontrolled
crash/reopen path, and a K4-gated conservative Uncontrolled restart that emits
\texttt{Unknown(Recovery)}. A separate six-record crash/reopen execution
terminalizes a durable successful Outcome as Commit while K4 remains in
recovery mode, without invoking the adapter again. It checks exact cuts,
prefix reuse, terminal phase, recorded evidence, and capability budgets. The
terminal cases therefore
exercise the uncertainty classification that motivates the work, not only
ordinary successful record processing.

This is kernel-in-the-loop trace-conformance evidence, not whole-program
refinement. The gate closes the selected broker append/replay path, but record
translation, Rust control flow outside that path, byte decoding, CRC,
filesystem and \texttt{fsync}, adapters, transport, service, and deployment
remain ordinary or trusted code. A bug in those components can still prevent
the gate from seeing a record or violate the assumptions under which the typed
record was produced.

K4-I1 is the fail-closed submission profile built on this gate. It requires an
immutable admission manifest and `open_with_gate` for every evaluation broker
construction, then emits a versioned attestation for gate/open equality,
preview/commit pairing, replay, and recovery closure. The retained 100-request
run has 47 broker opens and gates, 1,573 preview/commit pairs, 675 replays, 40
finished recoveries, and 7 verified resumes. This makes the submitted
evaluation configuration auditable; it does not promote the surrounding Rust,
byte WAL, or adapters into a whole-program refinement.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate four questions: whether the executable protocol behaves safely at
instrumented crashes (RQ1), what durable mediation costs in a local reference
implementation (RQ2), what proof surface each adapter instance requires (RQ3),
and which durable evidence justifies each recovery classification (RQ4).
Testing assesses the Rust implementation on measured traces; it does not
replace the refinement proof.

\subsection{RQ1: Does the reference protocol behave safely under crashes?}

\paragraph{Method.}
The fault harness crosses three executable adapter classes
(\Idempotent, \Deduplicated, and \Uncontrolled) with seven deterministic crash
sites: after authorization, preparation, arming, start, invocation, outcome,
and terminal append. This yields 21 adapter/crash-site cases. Oracles check
terminal uniqueness, attempt bounds, authorization ancestry, the expected
terminal, and abstract effects. Additional tests interrupt recovery, inject a
stale delivery after retry, and exercise the append-gate order and fail-closed
behavior. The current Rust suite contains 32 passing tests, including the
class-sensitive recovery-resume and gate fail-closed checks.

\paragraph{Answer.}
All 21 cases passed all five oracles, and stale delivery was rejected without
creating another terminal or effect. In the lost-reply \texttt{AfterInvoke}
case from Figure~\ref{fig:timeline}, the retry-safe adapters performed two
physical invocations but retained one abstract effect. \Uncontrolled performed
no recovery invocation and ended \Unknown. These results show that the
reference implementation operationalizes the intended distinctions at the
instrumented boundaries; they do not cover crashes inside the synchronous
adapter call or prove arbitrary Rust executions correct. The retained K4-I1
submission profile additionally reports 47 gated broker opens, 1,573
preview/commit pairs, 675 replayed records, 40 completed recoveries, and 7
verified resumes, so the reported evaluation itself did not silently fall back
to the ungated opener.

uses five warmups, 30 measured repetitions, 500 primary requests and 50 retry
requests per repetition, a 25-ms inter-run gap, and CPU affinity to one logical
processor. Table~\ref{tab:rq2} reports means and intervals for the retained

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Complementary deployment layers}

Agent-oriented confinement and effect recovery protect different dimensions.
AgentBound uses declarative MCP-server capabilities and container mechanisms to
restrict files, environment variables, and network endpoints
\cite{buehler2026agentbound}. Such a sandbox could help realize the resource
scope and handle-exclusivity assumptions of our broker. Our protocol, in turn,
specifies what a permitted effect means over time when invocation, external
linearization, delivery, persistence, and crash do not coincide. The artifact
does not verify this proposed deployment composition.

A realistic deployment would place protected credentials only in the broker or
adapter process, route service access through authenticated endpoints, and
bind the adapter descriptor to durable admission metadata. Those engineering
controls are necessary to translate closed-model mediation into production
mediation; the current theorem cannot substitute for them.

\subsection{What is specific to agents?}

The lost-reply ambiguity and its standard remedies generalize to RPC, workflow,
and dual-write systems. The AI-agent setting motivates a particular joint
contract: requests and parameters are selected at run time, authorization is
fine-grained and may be revoked or budgeted, tool effects range from reads to
uncontrolled mutations, physical attempts are hidden behind one apparent
action, and the terminal conclusion is consumed by a planner that can trigger
more effects. Our contribution is this authorization-to-effect composition,
not a claim that agents changed crash theory. The same contract may benefit
non-agent orchestration systems with heterogeneous external activities.

\subsection{Limitations and next refinement steps}

The verified execution model is serialized and fail-stop. It proves safety for
finite histories, not progress. Typed WAL frames abstract away bytes, checksum
implementation, flush ordering, filesystem semantics, and hardware durability.
Capability policy correctness and least privilege are trusted. The formal
closed interface rules out bypass by construction; it is not an OS sandbox
proof. Adapter contracts and service behavior are assumptions until a concrete
adapter implementation is refined to them. Deduplication additionally depends
on durable key retention.

Protected/contextual composition is complete for the modeled \Idempotent and
\Deduplicated instances; \ReadOnly currently stops at the adapter/WAL/Broker
boundary. The request-family theorem is coverage-conditioned, and production
code does not synthesize its coverage members. K4-A4 now covers explicit
crash/recovery control, durable-success Commit, and the class-sensitive resume
guard for the eight supported record kinds, but omits \texttt{Revoke} and byte
persistence. K4-I1 additionally makes the gate mandatory for the retained
submission evaluation and records its finite recovery closure. The Rust broker,
byte WAL, filesystem, adapter, and transport are not whole-program refinements.
The checked gate accepts the broker's recovery \Commit only for an exact durable
successful Outcome; otherwise an Armed \Uncontrolled request is closed with
\texttt{Unknown(Recovery)}, while retry-safe classes resume only under their
proved predicate.
The theorem also does not show that a durable terminal was
successfully returned to the agent, and it provides neither concurrent
multi-request semantics nor global external-effect linearizability.

The highest-value next implementation steps are therefore to extend the bridge
to \texttt{Revoke}, refine byte-WAL and filesystem behavior under explicit
platform assumptions, and then tighten record translation and deployment
isolation. Concurrency and liveness should come only after this single-request
implementation boundary is tighter. Moving record translation behind a smaller
checked interface is a parallel hardening step.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Agent authorization, confinement, and governed execution}

AgentBound combines declarative MCP-server capabilities with container-based
enforcement and evaluates resource confinement over a large server corpus
\cite{buehler2026agentbound}. CaMeL separates trusted control flow from
untrusted data and applies capabilities at tool calls to resist prompt
injection~\cite{debenedetti2025camel}. Guardians proposes verifying structured
agent workflows before execution~\cite{meijer2025guardians}. These systems
focus on which action, resource, or data flow is permitted. They do not define
a durable crash/retry protocol connecting one logical request, several physical
attempts, and a terminal external-effect interpretation.

Proof of Execution binds authorization, an enforced path, durable effects, and
replay context into a governed agent execution object
\cite{rhodes2026poe}. It is closer to our historical goal than resource
sandboxing alone and rules out a broad novelty claim for connecting agent
authorization to an effect trace. Its reported semantics validate a completed
causal trace; they do not provide our WAL recovery protocol, four adapter
classes, explicit \Unknown outcome for ambiguous invocation, or a
proof-assistant artifact. Conversely, our work does not provide its
cryptographic attestation and replay context.

Capability systems such as Capsicum establish practical resource confinement
\cite{watson2010capsicum}, while seL4 demonstrates much stronger
implementation refinement and security guarantees at an operating-system
boundary~\cite{klein2009sel4}. They are precedents for isolation and
machine-checked enforcement, not substitutes for an external-effect recovery
contract.

\subsection{Durable workflows and exactly-once mechanisms}

Temporal reconstructs durable workflow state by replaying event histories, but
its Activity guidance acknowledges that an external activity may be called and
then retried, motivating idempotent activity design
\cite{temporal2026workflow,temporal2026activity}. Reliable State Machines
provide fault tolerance by construction for cloud services
\cite{mukherjee2019rsm}. Beldi uses logs, intent tables, and runtime protocols
for transactional stateful serverless workflows~\cite{zhang2020beldi}. RIFL
uses unique RPC identifiers and completion records to obtain linearizable
exactly-once operations~\cite{lee2015rifl}. These systems demonstrate that
durable replay and deduplicated operations are mature. Our distinction is the
machine-checked link from a durable capability decision and physical mediation
to a class-relative terminal conclusion at a heterogeneous tool boundary.

Idempotency-key APIs expose one deployed form of the \Deduplicated contract.
The IETF document is an expired Internet-Draft rather than a completed standard
\cite{jena2025idempotency}; Stripe documents a concrete API whose keys can be
removed after a retention interval~\cite{stripe2026idempotency}. These examples
reinforce that uniqueness is service-owned and retention-bounded. Our theorem
therefore treats stable-key atomicity and lifetime as premises, not properties
created by the broker.

\subsection{Machine-checked crash and distributed systems}

Machine-Checked Dual-Write Recovery formalizes the indistinguishability of
post-send crash states and conditional escapes using sink acceptance, fencing,
deduplication, and evidence lifetime~\cite{andreakis2026dualwrite}. It rules
out novelty claims for either the ambiguity observation or machine-checked
deduplication itself. Our composition adds durable capability ancestry, a
broker terminal vocabulary, four adapter classes, closed-interface mediation,
and contextual refinement through Journal and typed WAL. In the other
direction, we do not prove its more general information bounds, fencing, or
finite-retention results.

FSCQ applies Crash Hoare Logic to a verified file system
\cite{chen2015fscq}; Argosy proves recovery refinement for layered storage
systems~\cite{chajed2019argosy}. Disel and IronFleet establish
machine-checked distributed-protocol and implementation precedents
\cite{sergey2018disel,hawblitzel2015ironfleet}. These works are substantially
stronger within their storage or distributed-system implementation boundaries.
They do not connect an agent's durable capability authorization to
adapter-specific protected tool effects.

\subsection{Synthesis}

Based on the sources reviewed through August 10, 2026, we are not aware of
prior work that machine-checks this particular coverage-conditioned composition:
durable capability ancestry, fail-stop attempt and recovery history, a
four-class adapter contract with explicit uncertainty, closed-interface
mediation, and protected-effect refinement at an AI tool boundary. This is a
qualified synthesis claim, not a claim that its building blocks or crash
ambiguity are new. It must be refreshed against work appearing before the
submission deadline.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Authorization can prove that the append in our running example was permitted;
it cannot prove what happened after the service acted and its reply was lost.
Authorization-to-effect consistency supplies the missing contract: durable
authority and attempt provenance determine which evidence belongs to the
request, while the adapter law determines whether recovery may retry, commit,
fail, or only preserve \Unknown. Our machine-checked protocol connects these
decisions through Journal and typed-WAL recovery to covered, mediated protected
effects under explicit premises. The verified record kernel and
kernel-in-the-loop broker traces make part of that boundary executable, while
the Rust runtime, byte persistence, adapters, and deployment remain outside
whole-program refinement.
