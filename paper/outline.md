# FSE 2027 Paper Blueprint

Status: revised narrative plan. The paper does not use `ProveAI` as a system
name. Internal milestone names such as T6-DD5 and K4-I0 are evidence labels,
not the paper's narrative structure.

## Working title

**From Authorization to Effects: Machine-Checked Recovery Semantics for AI
Tool Calls**

This title makes the research gap explicit. The paper is not claiming a new
general crash-recovery theory or a verified production runtime. It studies the
semantic connection between an authorized agent request and the external
effect that may survive a crash.

## Research questions

The introduction should frame two scientific questions, following the
correctness-definition-first style of verified-software papers:

1. **Correctness question:** What may an agent tool broker soundly conclude
   about an authorized external effect after crashes make its outcome
   ambiguous?
2. **Engineering question:** How can a broker enforce this contract across
   authorization, durable storage, retries, heterogeneous tools, and a
   protected service, and how much of that design can be connected to an
   executable implementation?

The four evaluation RQs later assess the evidence for these two larger
questions; they should not replace them in the introduction.

## Core argument

The paper should advance one argument in five steps:

1. Agent security mechanisms can correctly authorize and confine a tool call
   while leaving its post-crash effect unresolved.
2. Existing crash-recovery mechanisms remain useful, but their local
   guarantees do not by themselves connect an agent's logical request,
   historical authority, physical retries, and the external effect on which
   later planning relies.
3. We define **authorization-to-effect consistency**, a correctness contract
   requiring durable authorization ancestry, attempt causality, recovery-
   stable logical outcomes, and adapter-relative external-effect semantics.
4. We design and machine-check a durable broker protocol that satisfies this
   contract across Broker, Journal, typed WAL, adapter, and protected-service
   models under explicit serialization, persistence, coverage, and closed-
   interface assumptions.
5. We narrow the model/implementation gap with a verified executable record
   kernel, kernel-in-the-loop checks of real broker-generated traces, systematic
   fault injection, and cost measurements, without claiming whole-Rust
   refinement.

The story is therefore not "AI makes crashes different." The crash ambiguity
is classical. The AI tool boundary makes several independently familiar
obligations meet: dynamic capability-constrained requests, heterogeneous tool
effect contracts, physical attempts hidden behind one logical action, and a
terminal conclusion that returns to autonomous planning.

## Claim hierarchy

### Thesis claim

Authorization establishes that a tool call may occur; it does not establish
what effect occurred after a crash. A sound agent tool broker must derive its
terminal conclusion from durable authorization and attempt evidence together
with an explicit tool-effect contract, retaining `Unknown` when those premises
cannot justify success or failure.

### Protocol claim

The proposed protocol separates logical requests, physical attempts,
deliveries, durable observations, and terminal outcomes. It permits retries
only under ReadOnly, Idempotent, or Deduplicated contracts and prevents retry
of unresolved Uncontrolled effects.

### Mechanized claim

For every finite execution admitted by the serialized fail-stop model, each
physical invocation descends from durably acknowledged authorization, each
logical request has at most one durable terminal outcome, accepted results
have exact invocation and value provenance, and recovery extends committed
history only through crash-prefix-backed Commit records. For terminal requests
covered by valid operational/protected
execution members under the adapter, typed-persistence, and closed-interface
premises, every protected effect is mediated and the terminal outcome refines
the declared abstract-effect semantics.

### Executable-evidence claim

The artifact contains a verified executable kernel for arbitrary legal
`Authorize`, `Prepare`, `Arm`, `Start`, `Outcome`, and `Commit` sequences under
a well-formed manifest. Representative records generated by the ordinary Rust
broker are replayed through the compiled proof-erased kernel with exact prefix,
LSN, terminal-state, and budget checks. This is trace-conformance evidence, not
whole-program refinement.

### Qualified novelty claim

The novelty is the machine-checked composition of durable capability ancestry,
crash/recovery history, adapter-specific retry semantics, complete mediation
in a modeled protected interface, and external-effect refinement at the AI
tool boundary. Capability systems, WAL recovery, idempotency keys,
deduplication, `Unknown`, and recovery refinement are not individually new.

## Section plan

FSE permits 18 pages of text and figures. Target 17.5 pages before final
formatting.

### 1. Introduction (1.5 pages)

**Purpose:** Establish the authorization-to-effect gap and state the answer
before introducing machinery.

Open with one authorized `append_report_entry` request. The service appends the
record, its reply is lost, and the broker crashes. A resource sandbox has done
its job, yet the recovering broker cannot safely report `Fail`, report
`Success`, or retry without knowing the tool contract.

Explain why this matters specifically at an agent tool boundary: the request
and parameters are selected dynamically, tools have heterogeneous effect
semantics, authorization is scoped and historical, and the terminal result can
drive subsequent autonomous actions.

State the two research questions, introduce authorization-to-effect
consistency, summarize the broker approach, and give the result at one-
paragraph resolution.

End with three contributions:

1. a correctness contract and adapter-classified recovery semantics for
   authorized AI tool effects;
2. a compositional machine-checked protocol theorem from durable authority to
   protected effects, with its coverage and interface boundary; and
3. a verified executable record kernel plus kernel-in-the-loop integration,
   fault-injection, proof-effort, and runtime evidence.

Do not lead with TLA+, Verus, WAL, or obligation counts. Do not claim that the
underlying crash ambiguity is novel.

### 2. The Authorization-to-Effect Gap (1.5 pages)

**Purpose:** Define the research problem and establish why existing local
mechanisms do not already imply the desired result.

#### 2.1 Agent tool boundary

Introduce the agent, capability/policy issuer, broker, durable store, adapter,
and protected service. Identify the logical request returned to the agent and
the physical attempts issued to the service.

#### 2.2 Authorized append, lost reply

Give the event timeline:

```text
Authorize -> Prepare -> Arm -> Start -> Invoke
                                  service linearizes
                                  reply is lost -> Crash
```

Show what each component knows after recovery. Use the same example throughout
the paper.

#### 2.3 Why local building blocks are insufficient

Use a compact comparison table:

- capability/sandboxing proves which resource may be accessed;
- WAL/replay preserves broker-local history;
- workflow retry reissues work under an application assumption;
- idempotency keys or deduplication constrain a cooperating service; and
- none alone proves that one terminal agent conclusion corresponds to the same
  authorized logical request and protected effect across the whole history.

The point is composition, not failure of traditional techniques.

#### 2.4 System assumptions and non-goals

State serialized writer/executor, finite executions, fail-stop crashes,
well-formed configuration, typed persistence, closed record language, and the
closed protected interface. Immediately exclude liveness, Byzantine faults,
policy correctness, global multi-request linearizability, and production OS or
network verification.

**Transition:** The gap calls for a correctness contract before it calls for an
implementation.

### 3. Authorization-to-Effect Consistency (1.5 pages)

**Purpose:** Define what correct recovery means. This is the conceptual
contribution and should be understandable without proof-assistant syntax.

#### 3.1 Historical safety obligations

Define:

- durable authorization ancestry for every physical invocation;
- scope and capability-budget conservation;
- exact request/attempt causality for accepted deliveries;
- rejection of stale or mismatched responses;
- unique durable terminal outcome per logical request;
- monotone committed history across recovery, crash-prefix provenance for any
  recovery Commit, and endpoint equality when no recovery Commit occurs; and
- terminal value/failure provenance.

#### 3.2 External-effect obligation

Distinguish broker history from the protected effect. Define model-level
complete mediation and per-request effect refinement. Explain why WAL
correctness alone cannot establish either property.

#### 3.3 Adapter-relative recovery

Give the four-class table:

| Class | External law | Recovery permission | Unresolved conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| ReadOnly | no protected mutation | repeat observation | no mutation claim |
| Idempotent | repetitions refine one mutation | retry within budget | `Unknown` if evidence remains insufficient |
| Deduplicated | stable key selects one durable service decision | retry/reconcile with the same key | `Unknown` if the contract cannot resolve it |
| Uncontrolled | no retry law | do not retry after arming | `Unknown` |

State explicitly that the contract does not promise physical exactly-once.
`Unknown` is the sound consequence of insufficient durable evidence, not an
arbitrary fallback branch.

**Novelty:** A single contract connects authority, history, recovery outcome,
and external-effect semantics at the agent tool boundary.

### 4. Durable Effect-Broker Protocol (2.25 pages)

**Purpose:** Show how the design maintains the contract.

#### 4.1 Architecture and trusted boundaries

Figure 1 should show agent, broker, Journal/WAL, adapter, and protected service.
Use different visual treatments for machine-checked model, verified executable
kernel, tested Rust reference code, and trusted/unverified deployment.

#### 4.2 Logical request lifecycle

Explain immutable request IDs, capability binding and budget, `Authorize`,
`Prepare`, `Arm`, physical `Start`, invocation IDs, delivery acceptance,
`Outcome`, and terminal records. Explain why authorization and Start must be
durable before Invoke.

#### 4.3 Recovery rules

Show how replay reconstructs state, how unmatched Start becomes ambiguity, how
attempt IDs reject stale delivery, and how each adapter class permits or
forbids retry. Walk the motivating append through Deduplicated and
Uncontrolled outcomes.

#### 4.4 Design invariants

Summarize the invariants the proof will consume. Avoid theorem IDs here.

**Transition:** Local rules are credible only if they compose through storage
and the protected service.

### 5. Mechanized Composition (3.75 pages)

**Purpose:** Establish the strongest scientific result and its precise scope.

#### 5.1 Semantic layers and observations

Define the Broker transition system, atomic Journal, typed WAL, operational
adapter, protected-service execution, and storage-parametric context. Define
only the trace projections needed to state the theorem.

#### 5.2 Broker history and persistence refinement

Present Broker invariants, Journal-to-Broker simulation, typed-WAL-to-Journal
simulation, contextual replacement, and recovery-history preservation. State
the general prefix-extension theorem and crash-prefix durable-success law
before presenting exact equality as the no-recovery-Commit corollary. Make
clear which results quantify over every admitted finite history.

#### 5.3 From attempts to protected effects

Explain adapter rely/verification, independently generated protected-service
executions, event coupling, prefix-indexed effect-state agreement, and derived
complete mediation. This is where the proof goes beyond a large decision tree.

#### 5.4 Main theorem

State one paper-facing theorem with assumptions beside conclusions. Visually
separate:

- unconditional-on-coverage Broker/storage history guarantees;
- coverage-conditioned protected-effect guarantees;
- complete protected/contextual instantiations for Idempotent and
  Deduplicated;
- ReadOnly's adapter/WAL/Broker-only boundary; and
- Uncontrolled's no-retry and `Unknown` result.

Do not imply that production code automatically constructs the DD5 coverage
family.

#### 5.5 Non-vacuity and assurance

Use the concrete Idempotent and Deduplicated crash/retry executions to show the
premises are inhabited. Include H2's 26-event durable-success recovery Commit
witness, which falsifies the old stuttering premise while extending committed
history from length zero to one. Summarize the retained verification result
(72/72 targets, 1,511 non-duplicated obligations) and source/toolchain integrity
controls as assurance evidence, not as the contribution itself.

### 6. Executable Realization and Refinement Boundary (1.25 pages)

**Purpose:** Answer how far the theorem reaches into running code.

#### 6.1 Rust reference broker

Describe generated request IDs, manifest binding, capability budget, byte WAL,
torn-tail recovery, one executor slot, adapters, and terminal retrieval. Call
it a reference implementation consistently.

#### 6.2 Verified executable record kernel

Explain that K4-A2 proves the six ordinary record types and K4-A3 extends the
same append invariant to Fail and reason-specific Unknown under a well-formed
manifest, including exact terminal evidence, Journal projection, budget
materialization, LSN/cut agreement, and terminal-state reflection. K4-A4 adds
executable Online/Crashed/Recovering control, a durable-success recovery
Commit witness, and a class-sensitive resume guard; report its 365 verified
obligations precisely.

#### 6.3 Kernel-in-the-loop integration

Explain that K4-I0 injects an AppendGate into the real Rust broker append and
replay paths. Every record passes K4 preview before the WAL write and the
kernel is committed only after the durable LSN; reopening replays the full
prefix. The harness covers ordinary Commit paths, conclusive Fail, four
reason-specific Unknown paths, ordinary restart, a conservative K4-gated
restart producing Unknown(Recovery), and a six-record K4-gated durable-success
recovery Commit without another adapter invocation. Add K4-I1: immutable
manifests and `open_with_gate` are mandatory for every submission-evaluation
broker, with attested gate/open, preview/commit, replay, and recovery closure
counters. Present both as concrete trace/evaluation conformance evidence, not
whole-program Rust refinement.

#### 6.4 Remaining gap

Use a boundary table. `Revoke`, Rust control flow outside the gate, record
translation, byte decoding, CRC, filesystem/fsync,
crash runtime, adapters, transport, and deployment are not covered by
whole-program refinement. This subsection should appear before evaluation, not
be buried in threats to validity.

### 7. Evaluation (3 pages)

**Purpose:** Test whether the distinctions in the contract are operational,
reproducible, and affordable.

State each RQ and its answer before procedural details.

#### RQ1. Does the reference protocol behave safely under crashes and retries?

Report three adapters by seven crash sites, recovery interruption, terminal
uniqueness, authorization ancestry, retry bounds, effect oracles, and stale-
delivery rejection. Tie `AfterInvoke` to the motivating example. Label this
executable evidence, not proof of Rust refinement.

#### RQ2. What does durable mediation cost?

Compare direct invocation, journaled at-least-once, and mediated execution.
Report latency, throughput, bytes, flushes, recovery, and retry costs. Discuss
storage-path sensitivity and keep conclusions local to the measured setup.

#### RQ3. What is the proof-engineering cost of a new adapter?

Report source/proof size, reusable versus adapter-specific lemmas,
non-duplicated obligations, and verification time. Keep person-hours
unavailable rather than inventing them.

#### RQ4. Which durable evidence justifies Success, Fail, retry, or `Unknown`?

Present the 18-cell adapter/fault-window classification. This is the semantic
evaluation and closes the lost-reply example.

### 8. Discussion and Limitations (1 page)

**Purpose:** Explain implications without expanding the theorem.

#### 8.1 Complementary deployment layers

An AgentBound-like sandbox can plausibly realize resource confinement and
handle exclusivity; this protocol supplies temporal effect semantics. The
composition is proposed, not verified in the artifact.

#### 8.2 What is AI-specific and what generalizes

Be explicit that crash ambiguity is general. The agent setting motivates the
combination of dynamic scoped authority, heterogeneous tool contracts, one
logical action over several attempts, and results consumed by later planning.
The contract may also apply to non-agent workflow/RPC systems.

#### 8.3 Limitations and next refinement steps

Reiterate serialization, safety-only results, fail-stop faults, coverage-
conditioned protected execution, no byte-WAL/filesystem theorem, no production
isolation, no result-delivery theorem, and no global multi-request
linearizability. Identify K4 coverage of `Fail`/`Unknown` and stronger Rust
integration as the closest implementation-refinement extensions.

### 9. Related Work (1.5 pages)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate that the composition, rather than its ingredients, is
the remaining contribution.

Organize by guarantee boundary:

1. agent authorization, confinement, and information flow: AgentBound, CaMeL,
   Guardians, and PoE;
2. durable workflows and exactly-once mechanisms: Temporal, RSMs, Beldi, RIFL,
   transactional outbox, and idempotency-key APIs;
3. machine-checked crash and distributed systems: Dual-Write Recovery, FSCQ,
   Argosy, Disel, IronFleet, and seL4; and
4. the authorization-to-effect composition established here.

For close work, compare the counted event, durable evidence, owner of
deduplication, bypass/interface premise, machine-checked result, and executable
boundary. End with a qualified synthesis claim, not an absolute `first` claim.

### 10. Conclusion (0.25 page)

Return to the authorized append. Authorization proves the attempt was allowed;
durable attempt evidence and the adapter contract determine what may safely be
concluded about its effect. Restate the machine-checked composition and the
verified-kernel integration in one sentence each, then state the whole-Rust
refinement boundary plainly.
